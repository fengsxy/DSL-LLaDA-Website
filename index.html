Method

From hard masks to continuous states.

DSL-LLaDA replaces binary masking with graded per-token uncertainty, then maps each noisy state back into the pretrained backbone without changing the 8B architecture.

01

Continuous Noise State

Inject per-token Gaussian noise at varying SNR into unit-sphere embeddings instead of using binary mask tokens.

02

Softmax Converter

Map continuous noisy states into soft mixtures of backbone embeddings without modifying the 8B architecture.

03

Joint SDE Denoising

Update all response positions jointly in continuous embedding space using second-order Heun integration.

04

Delayed Readout

Commit to hard discrete tokens only after continuous denoising reaches the final decoding step.

Results

Few steps, fewer failure modes.

We evaluate fluency, repetition, and output length together so neither early termination nor fluent repetition can masquerade as generation quality.

4 / 4

Few-step summarization

Best ROUGE-1 on XSum, CNN/DailyMail, PubMed, and arXiv at NFE 8 and 16 without length-control heuristics.

<9%

Continuous stability

Repetition remains below 9% across every tested NFE budget in the 200-prompt generation experiment.

>98%

Selective robustness

More than 98% of clean tokens remain unchanged at 10%, 30%, and 50% random corruption.

Scope

Where continuous 8B diffusion is strongest.

The clearest gains appear in low-step, source-grounded generation such as summarization and instruction following.

At NFE 16 or below, continuous SDE sampling avoids the severe repetition and early termination failures of iterative unmasking. The source document anchors the denoising trajectory and helps all positions refine jointly.

At higher NFE, the advantage narrows. Vanilla LLaDA can match or exceed SDE on long-reference tasks, while open-ended creative generation remains challenging because parallel updates can converge on repetitive semantic attractors.
